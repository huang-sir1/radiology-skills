# Venue voice and house style

Use this reference for final copyediting when the target venue is known. It converts an already drafted manuscript into the appropriate house voice without changing science, numbers, or claims.

## Workflow

1. Identify the venue family.
2. Apply the family voice and formatting rules below.
3. Preserve every number exactly; flag suspicious values rather than editing them.
4. If a rule is pending or not verified, write `VERIFY FROM GUIDE` instead of guessing.

## European Radiology

### Voice

- Concise specialist radiology voice.
- Prefer specific clinical question over broad disease background.
- Keep Introduction paragraphs short.
- Keep Discussion interpretation direct, then comparison, limitations, and clinical conclusion.

### Formatting and style

- Be consistent with British or American spelling; do not mix.
- Oxford comma is acceptable and suggested.
- Use very common radiology abbreviations without definition only when safe: CT, MR, MRI, PET, US, BI-RADS, LI-RADS, PI-RADS.
- Define subspecialty or study-specific abbreviations in the abstract and again at first main-text use.
- Format p values as a lower-case italic p in prose if possible, with spaces around the operator: `p = 0.01`, `p < 0.05`.
- Use a leading zero before decimals unless the value equals 1.
- Use two digits after the decimal for p values except when very small or near the significance threshold.
- Refer to completed study actions in past tense; use present tense for results being presented to the reader.

### Things to remove

- Generic disease-burden openings.
- Unsupported "trend" language for non-significant results.
- Abstract conclusions that add implications beyond the objective/results.
- Figure captions that repeat the entire main-text interpretation.
- Decorative table formatting.

## Nature Partner / npj

### Voice

- Make the manuscript intelligible to a non-subspecialist scientist.
- Reduce jargon; explain unavoidable technical terms.
- Keep abbreviations sparse.
- Make background, rationale, and main conclusion clear in the title and abstract.
- Use a broader significance frame, but keep the closing claim bounded by validation.

### Formatting and style

- Number references sequentially in order of first citation when following Nature-family style.
- Include article titles in references when the venue requires them.
- Keep acknowledgements brief and non-effusive.
- Include author contributions, competing interests, and data availability when required.
- Data availability should specify the minimal dataset needed to interpret, replicate, and build on the findings, with DOI/accession codes where available.

### Things to remove

- Specialist-only shorthand in the title/abstract.
- Local workflow details that do not support the main claim.
- Over-specific methods language in figure legends.
- Decorative or boxed figure language that does not help interpretation.

## NEJM

### Voice

- Clinical, exact, and restrained.
- Prefer "was associated with," "showed," "estimated," or "suggested" over causal verbs for observational designs.
- Reserve "significant" for statistical significance; describe clinical importance separately.
- Avoid novelty language unless the study truly changes practice or disease understanding.

### Formatting and style

- Abstract uses Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions, with a 250-word cap from the supplied guide.
- Report p values as two-sided unless the design requires one-sided testing.
- Use p values to two decimals when greater than 0.01, three decimals between 0.01 and 0.001, and `P<0.001` when smaller than 0.001.
- Report ratio-measure CIs for HRs, ORs, and RRs from log-scale computations when preparing forest plots.
- Report missingness for baseline and analysis variables; do not allow unexplained complete-case primary analysis when missingness is material.
- For survival claims, check proportional hazards before retaining HR language. If competing risks matter, avoid simple Kaplan-Meier claims for event-time distributions.
- In randomized trial Table 1, avoid p values for baseline comparison.

### Things to remove

- "Trend toward significance" or unsupported directional language.
- Unadjusted secondary/exploratory p-value claims when multiplicity was not prespecified.
- Causal verbs in observational imaging AI.
- Medical image edits or AI-assisted image processing that are not disclosed.

## Science

### Voice

- Broad, brisk, and concept-first.
- The title and abstract should be understandable outside radiology.
- Emphasize the scientific advance, resource, mechanism, or paradigm rather than a local model metric.
- Use concise subheadings that tell the reader what the evidence does.

### Formatting and style

- Keep a standard Research Article near 3000 main-text words, 3 to 5 display items, and about 50 main-text references unless the extended online format is justified.
- Place replication-level Materials and Methods in supplementary materials when following the supplied guide.
- Keep display-item legends brief.
- Make data availability and repository identifiers visible in the manuscript or acknowledgments as required.
- Ensure the cover letter states the main point, related submissions, presubmission discussions, prior sharing, exclusivity, human/animal assurances, and data availability/restrictions.

### Things to remove

- Specialist acronyms in headline positions.
- Figure captions that read like Methods sections.
- Claims that only compare AUCs without explaining why the result changes scientific understanding.
- Supplementary data omissions for numbers underlying figures.

## Lancet family

Default proxy: The Lancet Digital Health Information for Authors, April 2026. Apply this as the Lancet-series house-style default unless a supplied guide for a specific Lancet journal contradicts it.

### Voice

- Practice-facing digital health voice: clinical utility, implementation, safety, policy, and equity.
- Keep the method auditable, but make the implication readable to clinicians and health-system readers.
- Use transparent declarations rather than generic boilerplate for AI use, funding role, data sharing, and conflicts.

### Formatting and style

- Use British spelling and Lancet-style punctuation when polishing for this venue.
- Abstract uses Background, Methods, Findings, Interpretation, Funding, with a 300-word cap from the April 2026 guide.
- Original Articles from the supplied guide are up to 3500 words, or 4500 words for randomized controlled trials, with about 30 references.
- Include a Research in context panel with Evidence before this study, Added value of this study, and Implications of all the available evidence. Do not put references in this panel.
- For text style: use a single space after sentences, do not use bold for emphasis in text, use decimal points midline, and write out numbers one to ten unless used as measurement units or in figures/tables.
- Cite references sequentially in Vancouver numbering style.
- Use STARD, STROBE, PRISMA, CONSORT-AI, SPIRIT-AI, or other required reporting guidelines according to design.
- Data sharing statement must specify whether data/dictionaries will be available, what will be shared, timing, location, access criteria, and restrictions.

### Things to remove

- Undeclared AI assistance beyond basic grammar/spelling.
- Patient-identifiable examples, images, prompts, or unpublished material exposed to AI tools.
- Race/ethnicity language that implies fixed biology without justification.
- KM curves without numbers at risk when survival is reported.
- Dense, non-editable figure text in line art or forest plots.
