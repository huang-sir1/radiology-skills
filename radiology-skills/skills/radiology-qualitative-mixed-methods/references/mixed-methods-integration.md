# Mixed-Methods Integration and Discordance

Use this reference for `mixed-methods-integration` and for mixed-methods reporting handoff.

## 1. Integration passport

Freeze:

- the decision problem and why one strand alone is insufficient;
- qualitative and quantitative questions, priority and timing;
- sampling relationship and integration level; person/case linking key only for person/case linkage;
- where integration occurs: design, recruitment, data collection, analysis or interpretation;
- the frozen artifact/version from each strand;
- expected joint display or traceable integrated narrative and rules for discordance;
- the meta-inference and its maximum claim.

Do not call a project mixed methods merely because it contains numbers and text.

Integration need not use the same participants. Interview findings may build survey items for a new
sample; staff interviews may explain site-level workflow results; separate samples may contribute to
a shared construct. Record what connects the strands and why that level supports the meta-inference.
Use `NOT_APPLICABLE_PERSON_LINKAGE` when justified, retain each strand's population/unit and prohibit
individual-level associations or causal claims inferred solely from aggregate convergence.

## 2. Core designs

| Design | Required logic | Radiology example | Main failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| convergent | collect/analyse strands in a similar phase, then merge comparable constructs | merge measured report-turnaround distributions with observation/interviews about interruption and handoff | juxtaposing two Results sections without integration |
| explanatory sequential | quantitative result deliberately selects or shapes later qualitative inquiry | sample high/low/discordant AI-use cases to explain heterogeneous reader performance | inventing post hoc explanations or exposing outcome groups without privacy control |
| exploratory sequential | qualitative findings build a later measure, intervention or quantitative test | derive workflow constructs from observations, then develop and test a survey or implementation measure | treating themes as already validated variables or scales |

Embedded designs are acceptable when one strand has a clearly bounded supportive purpose. Name the
priority and do not overstate the embedded strand.

## 3. Joint display contract

Use the [joint-display-and-discordance ledger](../templates/joint-display-and-discordance-ledger.md).
At minimum each row contains:

1. construct/question, each strand's unit and declared integration level;
2. quantitative estimate, uncertainty and artifact locator;
3. qualitative evidence locator and analytic interpretation;
4. relationship: `CONVERGENCE`, `COMPLEMENTARITY`, `EXPANSION`, `DISCORDANCE` or
   `NOT_COMPARABLE`;
5. data-quality asymmetry and plausible methodological explanation;
6. bounded meta-inference, alternative explanation and next adjudication step.

A joint display is an analytic object, not decorative formatting. It must allow a reader to trace
the meta-inference back to both strands.

A transparent integrated narrative is also acceptable when it provides the same source-to-inference
trace and discordance handling. A table is useful but is not itself a requirement for valid mixed
methods. In an exploratory sequential design, freeze the qualitative-to-item construction trace
before later quantitative collection; do not require future quantitative results at the planning stage.

## 4. Discordance workflow

Never force consensus by majority, sample size or narrative preference.

1. verify that the constructs, units, time windows and populations are genuinely comparable;
2. check measurement, sampling, missingness, fieldwork and analytic quality separately;
3. test pathway/context heterogeneity—for example role, site, shift, modality, case difficulty,
   access to priors or AI availability;
4. seek negative cases and inspect whether the apparent conflict reflects different layers, such as
   measured time versus perceived workload;
5. classify unresolved conflict as a result and narrow the meta-inference;
6. propose additional data only when it can distinguish live explanations.

`DISCORDANCE_UNRESOLVED` is an acceptable conclusion. It is more defensible than silently
privileging one strand.

## 5. Claim ceilings

- Convergence can strengthen confidence that two methods address a similar bounded construct; it
  does not remove shared bias or establish causality.
- Complementarity expands understanding but does not imply agreement.
- Qualitative explanation of subgroup performance is hypothesis-generating unless the design
  supports stronger inference.
- An exploratory qualitative construct becomes a candidate item/domain, not a validated instrument.
- Integration cannot rescue invalid quantitative inference or an unsupported qualitative analysis.
- Patient/clinician acceptability does not establish clinical utility, safety, cost-effectiveness or
  general implementation success.

## 6. Reporting minimum

Report the mixed-methods rationale, design name, priority/timing, sampling relationship, strand-
specific methods, integration point, joint display or integrated narrative, discordance, meta-inference, limitations and any
design changes. JARS-Mixed and the NIH OBSSR guide are useful transparency frameworks, not
substitutes for a study-specific integration argument.

[Fetters, Curry and Creswell (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4097839/)
(accessed 2026-09-04) describes integration through connecting, building, merging and embedding,
and through narrative, transformation and joint displays. Choose the level and format that enact
the declared design; do not impose a patient-level database join on every mixed-methods study.
