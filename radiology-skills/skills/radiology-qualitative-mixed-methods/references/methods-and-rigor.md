# Qualitative Methods and Rigor

Use this reference for `scope-plan`, `fieldwork-protocol` and `analysis-audit`.

## 1. Evidence object before method label

Freeze the phenomenon, role, setting, time window and intended inference before choosing a method.
“Explore barriers” is incomplete until the decision and pathway are named.

| Evidence need | Suitable source | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| personal experience, meaning, reasoning or sensitive account | individual semi-structured or in-depth interview | recalled or narrated experience is not observed behaviour |
| shared norms, disagreement and interaction | focus group | group hierarchy, disclosure and conformity shape the record |
| work-as-done, interruptions, coordination and artefact use | non-participant observation or workflow ethnography | observation does not directly reveal motive; observer effects remain |
| verbalised search or decision process during a defined task | concurrent or retrospective think-aloud | reactivity and incomplete verbalisation limit natural-workflow claims |
| bounded categorisation of textual material | qualitative content analysis | frequency is not automatically salience or causal importance |

A combination may be justified, but every source must have a role in the analysis. More sources do
not automatically create triangulation or validity.

## 2. Purposeful sampling and adequacy

Record a sampling matrix: stakeholder role, care-pathway position, site, modality, seniority,
experience with the technology, shift/time and characteristics relevant to the question. Sampling
for maximum variation, criterion cases, critical cases or theoretical development requires a stated
rationale. Convenience recruitment may be described honestly but cannot be relabelled purposeful
after collection.

Choose one adequacy logic and operationalise it:

- **Information power:** review aim breadth, sample specificity, theory use, dialogue quality and
  analysis strategy. Reassess during data collection and record why the accumulated material is or
  is not sufficient for the bounded aim.
- **Saturation:** name the construct—such as code, meaning or theoretical saturation—and the method
  to assess it. Record analysis batches, new information, unresolved variation and the stopping
  decision. Do not use the word without an inspectable process.

Neither approach yields statistical representativeness. A narrow, information-rich sample may be
adequate for its aim while remaining non-transferable to other sites, roles or populations.

## 3. Fieldwork contract

For each data-collection channel record:

1. facilitator/interviewer/observer identity, training, role and relationship to participants;
2. setting, participant composition, recruitment and non-participation;
3. topic guide or observation framework version and how probes were used;
4. recording, field-note, transcription, translation, checking and redaction process;
5. whether images, reports, AI outputs or cases were displayed and their frozen versions;
6. interruptions, group power dynamics, deviations and safety escalation;
7. reflexive notes distinguishing what occurred from the researcher's interpretation.

Focus groups need a confidentiality warning because investigators cannot guarantee what other
participants disclose later. Think-aloud requires a defined task, prompt protocol and reactivity
boundary. Workflow ethnography requires sustained contextual observation; a brief site visit should
not be relabelled ethnography.

## 4. Select and execute the analysis

| Approach | Fit | Required decisions | Frequent invalid upgrade |
|---|---|---|---|
| reflexive thematic analysis | patterned meaning across a dataset with researcher interpretation | theoretical orientation, familiarisation, coding, theme construction/review, reflexive position | treating coder agreement as universal validity or themes as facts “emerging” untouched |
| framework analysis | applied, multidisciplinary questions needing case-by-domain comparison | analytic framework origin, indexing, charting, matrix revision and interpretation | reducing the matrix to counts without contextual reading |
| qualitative content analysis | systematic manifest or latent categorisation | unit, inductive/deductive logic, category construction, context and frequency role | equating mention count with importance, prevalence or effect |

Keep stable identifiers from source record -> excerpt/field event -> code -> category/theme ->
interpretation. Preserve versioned codebooks or reflexive memo history, analytic meetings, decisions
and disconfirming material. Software organises data; it does not perform interpretation or establish
rigor.

### Team analysis

State whether analysis is reflexive, consensus/codebook-based or reliability-oriented. If several
analysts code:

- declare whether coding is for interpretive dialogue, codebook refinement or formal agreement;
- do not compute an agreement statistic merely to signal rigor;
- retain substantive disagreement and its resolution;
- record whose interpretation had decision authority and how disciplinary/power differences were
  handled.

## 5. Credibility procedures are conditional

- **Negative/deviant cases:** actively test a theme's scope and revise it or explain the boundary;
  do not hide them as outliers.
- **Member checking:** define whether participants correct factual accounts, comment on a summary or
  co-interpret findings. Agreement is not a truth certificate and later disagreement is data, not
  failure.
- **Triangulation:** state whether sources converge, complement, expand or conflict. Multiple
  researchers or methods do not mechanically validate one another.
- **Audit trail:** preserve versions, decisions, excluded material, coding changes, reflexive memos
  and claim revisions.
- **Thick contextual description:** supports a reader's transferability judgment; it does not prove
  generalisability.

## 6. Radiology-specific audit questions

- Which image series, reconstruction, report, worklist state, prior examination, AI output and
  display context did participants encounter?
- Are patients, radiologists, technologists, physicists, referrers and operational staff sampled
  as distinct pathway roles?
- Are night/weekend shifts, interruptions, urgent findings, nondiagnostic examinations, rejected
  images and escalation work visible?
- Could hierarchy suppress criticism in a mixed-seniority focus group?
- Are simulated cases or prompted think-aloud sessions clearly separated from natural clinical
  work?
- Does a theme claim experience or workflow meaning only, or has it been improperly upgraded to
  prevalence, safety, effectiveness, implementation success or clinical utility?

For reporting, SRQR is broad and COREQ is scoped to interviews/focus groups. They improve
transparency but do not certify design quality. Read [source-registry.md](source-registry.md) before
claiming a guideline is current or mandatory for a target journal.
