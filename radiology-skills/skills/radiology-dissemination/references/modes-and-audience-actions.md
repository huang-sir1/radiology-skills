# Dissemination modes and audience actions

Choose a mode only after the audience, context and desired action are explicit. Do not convert one
mode into another by shortening text alone; the decision context and evidence order differ.

## Mode contracts

### Conference poster

- Open with one question and one defensible take-home message, then population/design, imaging
  measurement chain, result with uncertainty, limitations and next decision.
- Preserve cohort/split hierarchy, modality/sequence or phase, reference standard, analysis unit,
  denominators, missingness and external-validation status.
- A poster content architecture may be produced here. A slide deck remains `radiology-paper2ppt`;
  a new standalone plot or composite scientific figure remains `radiology-figure`.
- Verify the current meeting's size, orientation, template, disclosure, accessibility, copyright,
  AI-use and upload rules. An accepted abstract does not prove the result or authorize image reuse.

### Conference abstract

- Lock the submission category and current field/word/character limits before compliance claims.
- Methods and Results must align: same population, endpoint, unit, metric, analysis and denominator.
- Report numeric results and uncertainty when the design supports them; do not promise unavailable
  results, turn a protocol into completed research, or hide negative primary outcomes.
- Meeting acceptance, award eligibility and later journal publication are separate decisions.

### Plain-language summary

- Stand alone: why the question matters, what was done, who/what was studied, what was found, how
  certain it is, important limitations, what it does and does not mean, and where to learn more.
- Prefer concrete nouns, short sentences and explained technical terms. Readability scores are a
  diagnostic aid, not evidence that the target audience understood the text.
- Preserve consistency with the scientific source. Co-design or user testing is stronger evidence
  of comprehension than author judgment alone.

### Patient or public explanation

- State whether the material concerns screening, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment planning,
  workflow or research only. Explain false positives, false negatives and incidental findings when
  they affect interpretation.
- Separate research participation, return of aggregate results and individual clinical results.
  Do not imply an obligation or policy that has not been verified for the project and jurisdiction.
- Give an appropriate route for questions; never replace the responsible clinical/research team.

### Clinical evidence brief

- Name the clinical decision, current pathway, intended user, comparator and affected population.
- Separate technical performance, clinical validation, clinical utility, regulatory status and
  local implementation readiness. State workflow/resource and equity effects.
- This is a decision brief, not a guideline or permission to deploy. Recommendations require the
  authorized clinical/governance process.

### Policy brief

- Define the policy problem and affected groups; describe current context, evidence, uncertainties,
  policy options, benefits/harms/costs/equity, implementation considerations and monitoring.
- Distinguish research evidence, local administrative data, stakeholder values and author judgment.
- A policy option is not a mandate. Avoid advocacy language unless the requested product is
  explicitly an advocacy brief, and disclose interests and funders.

### Press and social media

- Lead with an accurate finding, not a stronger causal or clinical claim. Include study type,
  population, scale, evidence maturity, key limitation and source link.
- Use absolute quantities where possible; avoid “breakthrough”, “game-changing”, “first” and
  “clinically proven” unless directly verified and supportable.
- Verify embargo, sponsor, journal, meeting and institutional media rules. A draft does not authorize
  release. Preserve a correction/contact route and archive the released wording/version.

## Claim transformations

| Scientific source | Safer audience-facing transformation |
|---|---|
| Retrospective association | “was associated with” under the studied conditions |
| Internal model performance | “performed in the development/held-out sample”; no real-world claim |
| External validation | “was tested at the named external setting”; no universal generalisation |
| Reader study | specify readers, cases, reading design and endpoint; no patient-outcome claim |
| Silent prospective run | technical/local performance without influencing care |
| Active impact study | effect under the tested implementation, with design and uncertainty |

Never translate “statistically significant” into “important”, “accurate”, “safe” or “beneficial.”
