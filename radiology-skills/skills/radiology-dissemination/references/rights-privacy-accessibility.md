# Rights, privacy and accessibility for imaging dissemination

## Radiology image reuse gate

For every displayed image or derived panel record:

- source artifact and owner; modality, sequence/phase/view and acquisition context;
- patient/examination/series identity handling; orientation, laterality, slice/frame and scale;
- window/level, crop, resampling, registration, segmentation, annotation and enhancement;
- whether the image is representative, selected, synthetic, reconstructed or generated;
- PHI review of pixels, overlays, burned-in text, DICOM headers, filenames, paths and linked files;
- consent/governance basis, copyright/license, attribution and permitted audience/channel;
- alt text or long description appropriate to the communication purpose.

PowerPoint crops, black bars, masks and off-canvas placement do not permanently remove PHI.
Prepare a permanently de-identified derivative before insertion and inspect the final exported
package, notes, hidden objects and linked media. A public communication image is not a diagnostic
display; avoid implying diagnostic fidelity when the viewing environment is uncontrolled.

## Rights are not one field

Track separately:

1. copyright ownership;
2. license or permission for the exact reuse and adaptation;
3. attribution requirements;
4. participant consent and research governance;
5. privacy/de-identification;
6. venue or publisher reuse terms;
7. embargo and publicity timing.

Open access does not automatically mean unrestricted reuse. Fair-use/fair-dealing and institutional
policies are jurisdiction-specific legal questions; mark them `LIVE_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` and
escalate when material.

## Accessibility contract

- Use descriptive headings and a logical reading order.
- Provide text alternatives for meaningful images and describe the evidence, not decorative pixels.
- Do not encode categories, direction or significance by color alone; check contrast and legibility.
- Expand abbreviations and explain specialist concepts for the intended literacy level.
- Provide captions/transcripts for time-based media and an accessible alternate format when needed.
- Automated checking is not a complete accessibility certificate; report what was actually tested.
- WCAG applies directly to web content. Applying its principles to posters, PDFs or social images is
  useful, but do not claim formal WCAG conformance without testing the full delivered artifact.

## Release receipt

Report `SCIENTIFIC`, `PHI`, `RIGHTS`, `ACCESSIBILITY`, `VENUE`, `EMBARGO` and `AUTHORITY` separately
as `PASS`, `CONDITIONAL`, `FAIL` or `NOT_ASSESSABLE`, each with evidence, owner and next action.
