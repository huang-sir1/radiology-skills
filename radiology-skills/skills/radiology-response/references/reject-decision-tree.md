# Reject decision tree — appeal, transfer, resubmit, or re-target

Use this reference when a decision letter rejects the manuscript: desk reject, reject after
review, reject with encouragement, or a transfer offer. The deliverable within 48 hours is
**one chosen route plus the package that route and journal require**. Do not assume that an appeal
excludes point-by-point rebuttal: Nature's current
[appeal guidance](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/initial-submission) permits a rebuttal
letter addressing referees and/or editors point by point, whereas a desk-reject appeal without
reviews may need only a concise brief. The worth-appealing criteria and appeal-shape rules live in
`../SKILL.md` ("Reject decisions: appeal or move on"); this file adds the route decision and clock.

## Step 0 — Identify the decision type

Read the decision letter before the reviews; the type constrains which routes exist. Note
any expiry on offers or invitations — verify in the letter or with the editorial office.

| Decision type | How to recognise | Routes it opens |
|---|---|---|
| **Desk reject** | Rejected before review; editor-only wording (fit / scope / priority) | Usually transfer or re-target; appeal only for a concrete factual or procedural error under the journal's instructions |
| **Reject after review** | Full reviews attached, no resubmission signal | Appeal (contestable grounds only), strengthen & resubmit as new, transfer, re-target |
| **Reject with encouragement** | "We would consider a substantially revised version as a new submission" | Treat as a major revision with a reset clock — strengthen & resubmit here is the default |
| **Transfer offer** | Rejection + offer to forward to a sister journal, sometimes with the reviews | Accept transfer, or decline and re-target; any appeal stays at the original venue |

## Step 1 — Classify every point (first 12 hours)

Split the letter and reviews into atomic items with stable IDs (`E-#` editor, `R#-#`
reviewer; `action-mapping.md` convention). Put each in exactly one bucket:

- **Fatal as written** — fit, scope, novelty, priority, "not competitive enough." Not
  fixable by editing; decides against the venue, not against the paper.
- **Fixable with analysis/data** — external validation, calibration, reader study, leakage
  control, reporting gaps. Costed in Step 2.
- **Contestable** — demonstrable factual misreading, COI/bias signals, procedural error.
  The only fuel an appeal has; apply the worth-appealing criteria in `../SKILL.md`.

## Step 2 — Cost the fixable items (by 24 hours)

For each fixable item record: data/analysis needed, whether the data exists, honest effort
in weeks, and the producing skill (`radiology-stats` / `radiology-design` /
`radiology-figure` / `radiology-reporting`). Sum honestly:

| Total remediation cost | Implication |
|---|---|
| Days to ~2 weeks, data in hand | Strengthen & resubmit is realistic — same venue if encouragement wording exists, else a sister venue |
| ~1–3 months (new cohort / reader study) | Weigh against the project's driving deadline (graduation / promotion / grant); transfer or a one-tier-down re-target usually beats a 3-month gamble |
| Not feasible (data / ethics / cost) | The item becomes a bounded limitation; if the venue's bar requires it, re-target lower |

If a hard external deadline drives this project, state it by Step 2 and let it veto slow
routes — route selection is a scheduling decision as much as a scientific one.

## Step 3 — Choose the route and draft its letter (by 48 hours)

Pick exactly **one** primary route. Run a fallback in parallel only when it costs nothing
(keep the re-target ladder warm while an appeal pends — appeals seldom succeed; never wait
on one with empty hands).

| Route | Take it when | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Appeal** | Step 1 surfaced contestable grounds; not merely disagreement with a fit/priority judgment | Build the package required by the decision letter and current journal policy: concise brief for an editor-only error, or point-by-point rebuttal and permitted revised evidence after review; shape, tone and remedy rules are in `../SKILL.md` |
| **B. Accept transfer** | The offered sister journal grades as a fit (`radiology-journal/fit-grading.md`) | Verify whether the reviews travel with the manuscript (editorial office); reformat only what the new guide requires (→ `radiology-submission`); answer carried-over reviews in the new cover letter |
| **C. Strengthen & resubmit as new** (same venue or sister) | Encouragement wording, or cheap fixes + still-right venue | Execute the Step-2 plan; keep a commitment ledger per item (`response-audit-gate.md`); disclose "revised version of [MS ID]" in the cover letter and summarise changes — hiding the prior version risks desk rejection on process grounds |
| **D. Re-target** (downgrade or lateral) | Fixes are slow/infeasible, or the venue's bar is the problem | Rebuild the ladder with the reviews as ceiling evidence (→ `radiology-journal`); strip the old venue's shape (→ `radiology-writing`); rebuild the package (→ `radiology-submission`); reuse ledger rows as the new submission's self-checklist |

## The 48-hour checklist

| Window | Actions |
|---|---|
| **0–12 h** | Step 0 + Step 1: identify the decision type; split into atomic IDs; classify fatal / fixable / contestable |
| **12–24 h** | Step 2: cost each fixable item; name the driving external deadline; route shortlist with a one-line rationale per route |
| **24–48 h** | Step 3: pick the route; draft the policy-compliant package (appeal / transfer acceptance / resubmission cover note); open the fallback task list |

Do not spend the first 12 hours rebutting reviewers in your head — classify before persuading. If the
letter mixes types, resolve Step 0 against the most constraining wording and ask the
editorial office when ambiguous.

## Appeal letter brief (template)

Fill every field before drafting; delete fields that do not apply. (Same brief format as
`radiology-submission/assets/cover-letter-brief.template.md`.)

- Manuscript ID, title, decision date:
- Editor to address (handling editor; editor-in-chief if unclear):
- The decision being appealed (one sentence, quoted from the letter):
- Contestable ground(s) — factual misreading / COI signal / procedural error:
- Evidence per ground, with manuscript location (section/page/figure/table):
- Why the error materially affected the decision:
- Remedy requested (re-review, or reconsideration by an additional reviewer):
- Related submissions / prior versions / conflicts:

### Appeal letter skeleton

> Dear Dr [Editor name],
>
> We respectfully ask you to reconsider the decision on [MS ID, title]. We do not dispute
> the reviewers' overall assessment; we write because [one specific factual or procedural
> error] appears to have materially shaped the decision.
>
> [Ground 1 — the letter states X; the manuscript in fact reports X at section/page Y /
> Figure Z.] [Ground 2, if any — same shape: the claim, then the verifiable location.]
>
> Because [why the error was material], we ask for [re-review / an additional reviewer].
> We will address the reviewers' remaining points in full during any further consideration.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Corresponding author, on behalf of all authors]

Rules: keep the editor-facing argument as short as the case permits, stay non-accusatory and cite
every factual claim to a manuscript or evidence location. If the journal requires point-by-point
rebuttal, reuse stable IDs and the commitment ledger; include new analyses, data or revised material
only when permitted and verified. Do not appeal merely because the authors disagree with a fit or
priority judgment → `radiology-journal`.
