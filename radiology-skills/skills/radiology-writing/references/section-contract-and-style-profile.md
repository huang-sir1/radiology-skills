# Section contract and style profile

Use this reference to keep a section scientifically bounded and stylistically consistent across
multiple drafting rounds or authors.

## Section contract

Define this before prose:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Section and purpose | The one job this section performs in the paper |
| Inputs | Results, value keys, figures/tables, verified citations, guide constraints |
| Allowed claims | Claims directly supported by those inputs |
| Forbidden claims | Causal, mechanistic, novelty, generalisability, or utility claims not licensed by evidence |
| Required moves | Section-specific logical sequence |
| Validation | How a fresh reviewer can tell the section succeeded |
| Budget | Word, paragraph, citation, and display-item budget |

For each paragraph, inherit the section contract and assign one narrower job. If a proposed
paragraph requires evidence outside the contract, update the evidence first or remove the claim.

## Author style profile

Use only the author's own writing samples. Three or more samples from the same language/discipline
are preferred; with fewer, label the profile preliminary.

Extract:

- sentence-length distribution and rhythm, not a single target length;
- paragraph length by section;
- preferred reporting verbs, hedges, transitions, and citation integration;
- modifier density and degree of concreteness;
- register shifts across Methods, Results, and Discussion;
- recurring terminology that should remain stable.

Do not use style calibration to evade AI detectors or imitate another living author's identity.
It is personalization and consistency, not authorship substitution.

## Priority order

1. Scientific truth and reporting requirements.
2. Target-journal and discipline conventions.
3. Author's own style profile.

When these conflict, follow the higher priority and log the conflict once. For example, retain the
journal's required statistical syntax even if the author's older samples use another form.

## Fresh-reader gate

After drafting, review from a clean-reader stance and answer:

1. What is the section's main claim?
2. What evidence supports it?
3. What boundary prevents overinterpretation?
4. Does every paragraph advance that claim?
5. Does any sentence introduce a new thread that the section does not resolve?
6. Are terminology and cohort/model names identical to the project registries?

Return findings before sentence-level polish. Fixing voice cannot rescue a broken contract.
