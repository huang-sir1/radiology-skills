# Transfer modes, readiness and value evidence

## Invention disclosure triage

An internal disclosure should identify the technical asset, problem, distinguishing features,
contributors, dated development evidence, funding/contract context, public disclosures, dependencies,
scientific evidence and plausible uses. It is an input to institutional evaluation, not a patent
application or ownership determination. Preserve disputed or unknown facts for the TTO.

## Orthogonal readiness matrix

Never compress these into one maturity score:

| Axis | Example evidence | Claim boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Scientific validity | reproducible analysis, independent units, uncertainty, limitations | does not establish product or clinical value |
| Technology readiness (TRL) | prototype/integration demonstrated against framework-specific exit criteria | framework and relevant environment must be stated |
| Manufacturing readiness (MRL) | producibility, supply, process capability, quality and manufacturing controls | may be not applicable to pure software; does not equal TRL |
| Clinical validation | intended-use performance in target population | does not establish improved care |
| Clinical utility | active-use impact on decisions, workflow, safety or outcomes | implementation-specific; not marketing authorization |
| Regulatory/QMS | jurisdiction-specific classification, evidence and controlled lifecycle | requires qualified owner; not commercial adoption |
| Interoperability/operations | DICOM/IHE/PACS integration, latency, reliability, monitoring, support | local testing required; conformance statement is not deployment |
| Commercial/adoption | verified need, buyer/payer, procurement, economics and sustained use | interest/pilot is not adoption or willingness to pay |

NASA/DoD TRL and MRL frameworks were not designed as medical-device clinical evidence ladders. If
adapted, state the mapping, relevant environment, evidence and exit criteria; never award a level
from narrative confidence alone.

## Value-proposition evidence ladder

Separate:

1. `HYPOTHESIS`: assumed user, problem, benefit, buyer or business model;
2. `DISCOVERY_SIGNAL`: interview/observation or workflow evidence with source and sampling limits;
3. `BEHAVIORAL_SIGNAL`: access, pilot participation, data contribution or budget-owner engagement;
4. `ECONOMIC_EVIDENCE`: measured resource effect, price research, budget impact or cost-effectiveness;
5. `ADOPTION_EVIDENCE`: authorized procurement and sustained use under monitored conditions.

Record alternative solutions, non-use, manual workflow and incumbent vendor products. Avoid leading
interviews and separate user, decision maker, buyer, payer, IT/security and patient interests.

## Partnership and licensing map

For each transfer option—sponsored research, collaboration, evaluation license, software/data/model
license, startup, assignment or open release—record objectives, assets, background/foreground IP,
fields/territories, exclusivity hypothesis, data/model use, publications, confidentiality, support,
regulatory/quality responsibility, improvement rights, termination and unresolved authority. These
are diligence questions, not contract drafting or recommendations.
