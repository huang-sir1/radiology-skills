# Patent landscape, confidentiality and diligence controls

## Search protocol

Freeze:

- landscape question and technical boundaries;
- invention concepts, functions, components, outputs and alternatives;
- keywords/synonyms, assignee/inventor names, CPC/IPC classes and citation expansion;
- patent databases, jurisdictions, date/status fields, query strings and retrieval date;
- family definition, application/grant/status handling and legal-event source;
- non-patent literature sources and cut-off;
- screening, inclusion/exclusion, deduplication and family consolidation;
- claim-reading scope and qualified legal-review boundary.

Search titles/abstracts/claims and classifications where supported. A keyword-only map is generally
insufficient for technical boundaries. Patent-family counts, grants, applications, citations and
legal status are different constructs; do not add them without a declared unit.

## Mandatory disclaimer

Every landscape output states:

> This is an informational, reproducible landscape of the searched sources and dates. It is not a
> patentability, validity, inventorship, ownership or freedom-to-operate opinion. Patent databases,
> family/status data, translations and unpublished applications are incomplete or time-dependent.
> Qualified counsel must review claims, jurisdictions, dates and legal status for a legal decision.

## Confidentiality gate

Before any public abstract, manuscript, poster, repository, demo, press/social copy or partner pitch:

- list the exact information proposed for disclosure;
- compare with the internal disclosure and TTO instructions;
- record filing/protection/publication state without inventing it;
- verify authorized recipients, NDA/contract state and channel;
- remove confidential implementation detail and patient/data identifiers from the external version;
- retain a redline between confidential and non-confidential versions and an approval receipt.

Do not advise that an NDA guarantees protection or that a grace period applies; jurisdictions differ.

## Diligence evidence graph

Map each material claim to a source and owner across:

- unmet problem and intended user/action;
- asset architecture and reproducibility;
- scientific, clinical and workflow performance;
- prior-art/patent landscape and IP dependencies;
- data/model/code rights and provenance;
- regulatory, QMS, cybersecurity and privacy state;
- interoperability, deployment, monitoring and support;
- alternatives, stakeholder evidence, economics and market assumptions;
- team, contributors, funding/contracts and conflicts;
- open risks, stop rules and qualified-owner decisions.

A missing source remains `UNVERIFIED`; planned work remains `PLANNED`; user statements remain
`USER_ATTESTED`. Neither becomes verified diligence evidence through polished prose.
