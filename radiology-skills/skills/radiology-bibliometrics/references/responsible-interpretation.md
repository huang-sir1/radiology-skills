# Responsible interpretation and radiology claim limits

## Indicator-to-claim map

| Observation | Permitted interpretation | Not established |
|---|---|---|
| Publication count | indexed output under the declared corpus/counting method | productivity quality or completeness |
| Citation count/normalized citation | indexed scholarly attention under source/window/reference set | correctness importance patient benefit |
| Altmetric/event count | tracked online attention in covered channels | approval endorsement public understanding |
| Degree/strength/centrality | structural position in the specified network | leadership influence causality or quality |
| Cluster/community | algorithmic grouping under chosen data and parameters | a true discipline or stable frontier |
| Topic growth | increasing indexed records/terms under fixed method | novelty unmet need or successful innovation |
| Industry coauthorship | recorded affiliation collaboration | technology transfer adoption or commercial value |

## Responsible assessment rules

- Start from the decision purpose; do not let available metrics define merit.
- Use multiple complementary indicators and qualitative context. Keep article-, journal-, person-,
  institution- and country-level constructs separate.
- Do not use journal-level metrics to score an individual article or researcher.
- Allow assessed researchers or institutions to inspect identity and corpus errors when consequential.
- Report uncertainty, sensitivity and data limitations; avoid false decimal precision and league-table
  rankings when intervals or method choices overlap.
- Document incentives and gaming risks such as self-citation, salami publication, honorary authorship,
  citation cartels and strategic affiliations without accusing individuals from metrics alone.

## Radiology interpretation traps

- Conference-heavy computer-vision subfields and journal-heavy clinical radiology have different
  coverage; direct raw-count comparisons are often invalid.
- A model-development cluster is not a clinical-deployment cluster. Classify intended use and
  evidence stage from content or validated metadata, not “AI”, “validation” or “clinical” keywords.
- Large consortia can dominate coauthorship and country networks; show results with an explicit
  consortium/hyperauthorship sensitivity.
- Imaging modality and disease labels are multi-valued. One paper may contribute to several topics;
  do not force exclusivity unless the analysis question requires it.
- Publications or patents mentioning PACS, DICOM or prospective data do not prove interoperability,
  authorization or routine adoption.

Bibliometric outputs generate hypotheses and map attention. `radiology-frontier` evaluates a research
opportunity; `radiology-systematic-review` evaluates an evidence question.
