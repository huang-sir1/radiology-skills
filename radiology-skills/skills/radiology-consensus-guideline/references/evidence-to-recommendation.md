# Evidence to recommendation

Use this reference after the product, scope and decision questions are locked. Consensus can help a
panel interpret incomplete evidence, but it cannot turn expert agreement into measured effects or
certainty.

The systematic-review and EtD contract below applies to an evidence-based clinical recommendation.
For `research-priority/definition-consensus`, use a traceable item-generation/mapping/stakeholder
evidence packet and the formal-consensus protocol. Mark clinical-effect certainty and recommendation
strength `NOT_APPLICABLE_GRADE` when absent by design; do not require a therapeutic effect review to
define terminology or rank research questions. Any embedded empirical effectiveness claim still
needs its own appropriate evidence and may trigger the systematic-review lane.

## 1. Evidence handoff contract

Receive from `radiology-systematic-review`:

`review question and scope | protocol/registration/version | last search and surveillance state |
study-family/report/effect-row ledgers | eligible designs | risk-of-bias/applicability | synthesis and
heterogeneity | outcome-specific effects and uncertainty | certainty framework/result | subgroups |
evidence gaps | source locators and digest | claim ceiling`.

Return `EVIDENCE_PARTIAL`, `EVIDENCE_ABSENT` or `EVIDENCE_STALE` when the decision-facing scope,
population, modality, comparator, outcome, setting or currency does not match. Selected articles,
panel memory, an abstract-only table or a narrative citation list is not a systematic-review handoff.

## 2. Imaging question and outcome chain

For a test or imaging strategy define:

`population and pathway position -> index modality/test/protocol -> comparator/current care -> test
result and threshold -> management action -> patient-important benefit/harm -> resource/access and
equity consequences`.

Do not infer patient benefit from accuracy, image quality, workflow speed or model discrimination
alone. When direct outcome evidence is absent, expose every link, its evidence and uncertainty.

## 3. Evidence-to-decision criteria

Use the verified framework appropriate to the product. For a GRADE-style EtD record include:

- priority and exact perspective;
- desirable and undesirable effects in absolute as well as relative terms when possible;
- outcome-specific certainty and important indirectness;
- patient values/preferences and variability;
- resource requirements and certainty, cost-effectiveness when actually assessed;
- equity, acceptability and feasibility;
- subgroup or setting differences; and
- overall balance, recommendation type/strength and justification.

The panel judgment cites the evidence source and records uncertainty/dissent. Missing evidence is not
zero effect, no harm, equal values or acceptable feasibility.

## 4. Strength, certainty and consensus

Keep these fields separate:

| Field | Question | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| evidence certainty | how confident is the panel in the effect/evidence for each outcome? | percentage agreement as certainty |
| recommendation strength | how broadly should the stated action apply under the framework? | high certainty automatically means strong |
| consensus result | did this selected panel meet its prespecified agreement rule? | consensus proves truth or generalizability |

A strong recommendation with low/very-low certainty requires an explicit framework-based exceptional
rationale, such as catastrophic harm avoidance or near-certain equivalence of alternatives; do not
make it routine. When benefits/harms, values or feasibility remain unresolved, use a conditional,
research-only or no-recommendation state rather than confident wording.

## 5. Atomic recommendation contract

Record:

`Recommendation ID | question/scenario | target population/setting | action and comparator |
conditions/exceptions | strength | evidence certainty | effect/harms/burden | EtD judgments |
evidence locators | consensus method/result | dissent | implementation | monitoring | update trigger |
research gap | status/version`.

Recommendation wording must reveal whether it is evidence-based, consensus-based, conditional,
research-only or not supportable. Avoid “standard of care,” “must,” “always,” “never” and universal
numeric cutoffs unless the verified authority, evidence and context support them.

## 6. Evidence gaps

When evidence is absent or indirect, the panel may:

- state no recommendation;
- offer a transparently bounded consensus statement;
- define a research-only option or data-collection recommendation;
- specify implementation conditions and monitoring; or
- formulate a prioritized research question.

It may not hide absence behind citations, voting, prestige, local custom or a fabricated GRADE grade.
