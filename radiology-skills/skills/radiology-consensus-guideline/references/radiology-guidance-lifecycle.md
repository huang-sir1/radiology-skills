# Radiology guidance products and lifecycle

Use this reference to choose the correct artifact and keep it current. Names such as guideline,
appropriateness criteria, practice parameter, technical standard, white paper and consensus
statement do not confer authority by themselves.

## 1. Product distinctions

| Product | Primary job | Minimum method boundary |
|---|---|---|
| clinical practice guideline | recommend clinical actions across a defined pathway | systematic evidence review, transparent EtD, stakeholder/patient involvement and recommendation grading |
| appropriateness criteria | rate defined imaging/treatment options for clinical variants | scenario lock, evidence tables and explicit rating/disagreement method |
| practice parameter | describe reasonable performance/interpretation practice | declared developer authority, evidence/consensus process, field/external review and scope |
| technical standard | specify technical performance/QC expectations | medical physics/technical expertise, measurement basis, tolerances/applicability and update control |
| expert consensus statement | provide bounded guidance where evidence is limited/emerging | explicit evidence limits, panel/COI, consensus method and non-guideline claim boundary |
| priority/definition consensus | agree terminology, core sets or research priorities | representative stakeholders, prespecified item/consensus process and no clinical-effect upgrade |

An ACR Practice Parameter or Technical Standard is an ACR-governed product. This skill can emulate a
transparent research method but cannot call a local draft “ACR,” obtain endorsement or establish a
legal standard of care.

## 2. Radiology scope passport

Record:

- clinical decision and pathway position;
- population, age, pregnancy, acuity, prior imaging/treatment and relevant subgroups;
- modality, protocol, contrast/tracer, dose/count, sequence/phase/view, reconstruction and software;
- index test and alternative/no-imaging strategy;
- reader/referrer/technologist/physicist and facility expertise;
- diagnostic, therapeutic and incidental downstream actions;
- patient burden, radiation/contrast/sedation risk, access, equity, availability and cost;
- image quality, quantitative validity, failure/abstention and resource constraints; and
- versioned clinical/acquisition standards and local applicability.

For AI, include intended use, user, model/version/threshold, input availability, failure and human
oversight. Retrospective accuracy or expert enthusiasm does not establish a clinical recommendation.

## 3. Panel and stakeholder composition

Match composition to the decision: clinical experts, generalists/referrers, radiologists,
technologists, medical physicists, nurses/other implementers, methodologists, health economists when
needed, patients/public/caregivers and affected settings. Record geographical, practice, sex/gender,
career and resource-setting balance as relevant.

COI declarations are assessed and managed before question/item decisions, then updated. Record
recusal, restricted participation or exclusion by recommendation. Disclosure alone is not always
management; expertise alone does not erase a material conflict.

## 4. Consultation, implementation and monitoring

Publish or preserve the scope, methods, evidence, recommendation rationale, COI, dissent and
stakeholder comments/responses as authority permits. For implementation specify:

- target users and workflow location;
- required equipment, expertise, training and resources;
- barriers, equity/access consequences and adaptable versus non-adaptable components;
- audit/quality indicators and unintended consequences; and
- research recommendations tied to evidence gaps.

Implementation tools do not upgrade evidence. Local adoption should document deviations and context
rather than silently changing the recommendation.

## 5. Surveillance, update and retirement

Set owner, cadence and event triggers. Triggers include new pivotal evidence, safety signals,
withdrawn contrast/tracer/device, dose/protection changes, new modality or comparator, scanner/
reconstruction/software/AI changes, changed disease classification/treatment pathway, inequitable
implementation, infeasibility or loss of developer authority.

Record `CURRENT`, `UPDATE_IN_PROGRESS`, `PARTIALLY_SUPERSEDED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETIRED` with date,
scope and replacement. Do not leave contradictory versions simultaneously “current.” Urgent updates
still require transparent evidence, authority and later full review.
